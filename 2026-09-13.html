1. Who Cleans the Master’s House? Social Reproduction of/against ‘international relations’

MIL ｜ Carina Uchida ｜ 2026-09-12
展开摘要

Social reproduction has been spearheaded to configure the myriad of gendered and racialised ways society is maintained over time. Yet, social reproduction has seldom been applied directly to international relations, despite its analytical power to connect intimate practices to systemic orders. This stems from misconceptions of the international system as a high-level, structural entity devoid of mundane social processes. To refute these, often violent, assumptions, this article argues for a social reproduction of/against international relations to unravel the ways naturalised orders in the discipline are perpetuated and sustained. Using the practice and analogy of cleaning, this framework highlights the mechanisms of disciplinary maintenance on material and conceptual fronts, and how they mutually reinforce one another so the ‘international’ can remain rigid in its worldmaking. This article illustrates these processes as both exploitative and politically transformative, through the cases of Justice 4 Cleaners and the University College London higher education strikes (2018–2023).

2. Ontological Hierarchies and Biopolitical Borders: State Investment in Nonhuman Animal Rights

MIL ｜ Iver ‘Jay’ Johnson ｜ 2026-09-12
展开摘要

The growing alliances forming between State power and animal rights activists are creating a situation in which nonhuman animals are made to occupy a particularly ambivalent position within global politics. One where they remain resources to be raised, slaughtered, and consumed in some contexts, yet who are simultaneously posited (by States and activists alike) as important enough to justify the utilization of carceral, military, and immigration enforcement mechanisms for their protection in others. To explore this phenomenon, this article engages with specific sites where the confluence of animal protection activism, strategies of dehumanization, alliance with State power, and interspecies biopolitical practices converge. Through these sites, I seek to demonstrate how the alliance between State power and the animal rights movement acts to rewrite norms concerning belonging to national communities, global communities, or humanity itself. It is my contention that attentiveness to the interspecies nature of these practices demonstrates how nonhuman animals, their protection, and their killing are increasingly utilized to create, justify, and uphold a wider matrix of interspecies violence and control where practices of protection and killing are made increasingly difficult to disentangle or distinguish from one another.

3. State-led financialization of social care: variegation and regulatory mechanisms in Spain

RIPE ｜ David Palomera ｜ 2026-09-10
展开摘要

The social reproduction crisis is intensifying pressure on state institutions to provide affordable social care, even as welfare-state development increasingly unfolds through variegated neoliberalisation, including financialization. While the state-led financialization of social care infrastructure has been analyzed in liberal and continental welfare systems, its magnitude and mechanisms remain underexplored in Southern European contexts, traditionally characterized by familialist, residual, and outsourced social care provision. This article addresses this gap by examining the residential care sector in Spain in recent decades, with a focus on Catalonia. It shows that the universalization of long-term care as a social right has fostered rather than constrained financialization: Rights expansion has coincided with, and enabled, rising ownership concentration among large for-profit providers and international asset managers extracting high profits from the sector. Drawing on documentary, statistical, and interview evidence, the article argues that this concentration is driven by state-led financialization operating through four mechanisms: budget austerity; standardization, service size growth, and financial requirements; a procurement regime that disregards quality-of-care outcomes; and the reproduction of care-work precarity as a source of profit. By bridging social policy and political economy research, the article shows that the state is not a bystander to the financialization of social reproduction but a central institution.

4. Popular pressure, mock compliance or leadership as distraction: the UK’s approach to beneficial ownership transparency

RIPE ｜ Victoria Gronwald ｜ 2026-09-10
展开摘要

Since the 2008 financial crisis, the United Kingdom (UK) has positioned itself as a global leader in beneficial ownership transparency, pioneering public registers of UK companies and of overseas companies that own real estate. This paper asks what is behind the UK’s leadership approach, in particular as it seems like a risk to its powerful wealth management center. Based on an analysis of consultation submissions and semi-structured interviews, the paper finds that the UK’s leadership occurred in a context of significant popular pressure and salience, which however cannot alone explain the leadership approach. Demonstrating that mock compliance does not fit the case either, this paper puts forward the concept of ‘leadership as distraction’. It describes when a country steers certain reforms to distract from other – more undesirable – ones, using the reputational benefits of leadership to shield other areas from reform. Understanding the dynamics of leadership as distraction opens up a different perspective on how governments reconcile pressures to abide by international standards with protecting important economic sectors.
